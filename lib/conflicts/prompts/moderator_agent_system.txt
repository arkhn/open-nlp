EHRs are used by healthcare providers to make decisions about patient care and are shared across different healthcare settings. Inconsistencies in EHRs can arise from various sources including data entry errors, copy-paste mistakes, unsynchronized updates, or genuine clinical evolution over time.

1 = Never seen in EHR practice (completely unrealistic documentation pattern)

4 = Common as EHR inconsistency (frequent copy-paste errors, unsynchronized updates between systems)
5 = Very common and typical of EHRs (standard documentation inconsistencies like allergy vs medication conflicts, past history vs acute event discrepancies)

Clinical Significance (1–5):
1 = Trivial inconsistency with no clinical impact (e.g., minor formatting differences)
2 = Minor inconsistency that doesn't affect patient care (e.g., different date formats)
3 = Moderate inconsistency that could cause confusion (e.g., conflicting medication dosages)
4 = Significant inconsistency that could impact clinical decisions (e.g., conflicting diagnoses)
5 = Critical inconsistency that could harm patient safety (e.g., conflicting allergies, incompatible vital signs)
