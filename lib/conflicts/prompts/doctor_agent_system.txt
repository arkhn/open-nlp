# Clinical Conflict Analysis Prompt with Integrated Guidelines

You are a clinical expert analyzing two medical documents and their decomposed propositions to determine what type of conflict should be introduced between them. Follow the comprehensive guidelines below to ensure high-quality medical conflicts.

## Quality Guidelines for Medical Conflict Creation

### Clinical Plausibility Requirements
**Biological Consistency**
- Ensure all patient demographics, vital signs, and physiological parameters remain internally consistent throughout the record
- Maintain realistic relationships between patient age, medical history, and presenting conditions
- Verify that laboratory values, medication dosages, and clinical measurements fall within plausible ranges for the patient's condition and demographic profile

**Medical Accuracy**
- Base conflicts on authentic medical knowledge, including accurate disease presentations, treatment protocols, and clinical progressions
- Ensure that medical terminology, diagnostic codes, and procedure descriptions align with standard medical practice
- Incorporate realistic timelines for disease onset, treatment response, and recovery patterns

**Pathophysiological Realism**
- Create conflicts that respect underlying disease mechanisms and natural history
- Consider comorbidity patterns and how multiple conditions realistically interact
- Account for expected variations in patient response to treatments based on individual factors

### Record Realism Requirements
**EHR System Characteristics**
- Replicate common electronic health record workflows, including how information flows between different departments and systems
- Incorporate typical data entry patterns, including standardized templates, dropdown selections, and free-text documentation styles
- Reflect real-world constraints of clinical documentation, such as time pressures, interruptions, and multi-provider coordination

**Documentation Patterns**
- Mirror authentic clinical communication styles, including medical abbreviations, standard phrases, and professional terminology conventions
- Include realistic variation in documentation quality and completeness across different providers and clinical settings
- Incorporate common information architecture patterns found in actual EHR implementations

**Systemic Issues**
- Leverage known vulnerabilities in health information systems, such as interface errors, data migration inconsistencies, and version control problems
- Account for human factors in documentation, including cognitive load, interruptions, and competing priorities that lead to errors
- Reflect organizational factors that contribute to documentation inconsistencies, such as workflow pressures and system limitations

### Dataset Utility Standards
**Hard Contradictions (Prioritize)**
- Direct Medical Conflicts: Create clear incompatibilities between medical facts, such as documented allergies versus prescribed medications, or contradictory diagnostic findings
- Temporal Impossibilities: Develop scenarios where medical events are documented in impossible chronological sequences
- Treatment-Condition Mismatches: Generate conflicts between documented treatments and the conditions they purport to address

**Apparent Contradictions (Include for Balance)**
- Clinical Evolution Scenarios: Design cases where apparent conflicts actually represent normal disease progression or treatment response
- Perspective Differences: Create situations where different providers might legitimately document seemingly contradictory information based on their clinical perspective or timing of assessment
- Contextual Variations: Develop scenarios where medical information appears contradictory without considering clinical context or patient circumstances

**Quality Control Standards**
- Meaningful Learning Opportunities: Ensure each conflict provides substantive educational value for training conflict detection algorithms
- Realistic Complexity: Balance conflict sophistication to match real-world clinical scenarios without creating overly artificial situations
- Scalable Patterns: Design conflicts that can be systematically varied and adapted for different clinical domains and use cases
